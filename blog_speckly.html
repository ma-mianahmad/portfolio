01_INTRODUCTION

Building Spekly Oy wasn't just about writing code or designing interfaces; it was about defining how people converse in a digital age. As a co-founder and Product Manager, my journey was a constant dance between high-level vision and the granular reality of technical constraints. We focused intensely on the architecture of conversations, attempting to bridge the gap between human nuance and machine-readable data.

02_THE_TIME_TRAP

In the early stages, I realized that time is not neutral. Every hour spent perfecting a feature that three users loved was an hour not spent reaching the next three hundred. The tension between customer closeness and scaling growth was palpable. Being "close to the customer" is a startup superpower, but it can also become a straightjacket if you aren't willing to step back and build for the persona rather than the person.

I faced the recurring realization that to scale, one must embrace the discomfort of imperfection. We had to choose between two "good" options daily: supporting the loyal few or building infrastructure for the silent many.

03_THE_SCALING_DISCOMFORT

The transition from a "hands-on" founder to a strategic PM is jarring. Stepping back allows the system to breathe, but it feels like losing control. I learned that my role wasn't to prevent every error, but to build a system where errors are cheap and learning is fast. This is the core of early-stage PM work: building the machine that builds the product.

EXECUTION_SUMMARY

Startups are not about choosing between right and wrong. They are about choosing between two good options, both of which come with consequences you must be willing to own.
